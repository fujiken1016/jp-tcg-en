What 82 Japanese TCG Lottery Entries Actually Returned

Written from inside Japan. First-hand log, June–September 2026. No affiliate links on this page.

Every guide to Japanese card lotteries tells you how to enter. Almost none of them tell you what entering actually returns, because almost nobody keeps the receipts. I do. This page is the log: 82 dated outcomes, five of them wins — and, more useful than the headline number, the failure modes that cost time and money even when you are doing everything right.

1. The numbers, and exactly what they count

Between 12 June 2026 and 5 September 2026 my ledger records 70 losing notifications covering 77 individual items, and 5 wins, according to the dated entries in that ledger (last recount: 6 September 2026). That is 82 outcomes I can put a date on.

What the percentage counts

Some care about what that 6.1% (※ shares in the table are computed from the 82 dated outcomes) is and is not:

  • It counts items, not applications. A single notification sometimes covered two or three products entered together.
  • It excludes an older batch of 8 losses logged in a separate section of the same ledger (7 of them Premium Bandai, resolved on 5 August). Including those gives 5 wins out of 90 = 5.6%, according to the same ledger. I am showing the higher of the two numbers so nobody can accuse me of picking the flattering one.
  • It is one person’s log (the entry mechanics are covered in what a Japanese lottery actually is). ※ It is not a market-wide hit rate, and there is no reason to think my entry mix is representative of yours.

The one takeaway from this table

The single practical takeaway: LivePocket is the closest thing to a universal inbox. If you enter Japanese card lotteries at any volume, a large share of your results will arrive from one sender, and it is worth filtering for it rather than reading everything.

4. Three retailers that never tell you that you lost

The companion page explains that silence usually means you lost. Here is what that looked like in practice, with the specific retailers.

The three, and what each of them does instead

  • Yodobashi.com emails winners and adds the item to their cart. Losers get nothing, ever. Checking email is not a method; you have to open the entry-results page on the announcement date.
  • Dragon Star publishes win/lose on the member lottery page and sends no losing mail at all. In my log this resolved four separate rounds. Waiting for email would have left all four permanently open.
  • Toreca Plaza mail order notifies winners only. Three consecutive rounds of mine ended with no message of any kind. After the third one I stopped treating it as ambiguous: announcement window passed with no mail equals loss, close it.

Why an unresolved entry is worse than a loss

There is a second-order cost here that matters more than the losses themselves. An entry you never resolve stays on your list as a maybe, and every maybe carries a payment deadline you might be about to miss. Unresolved entries are the actual expense.

5. Results arrive before the announced date

※ Three separate LivePocket results in my log, according to the timestamps on the emails, landed ahead of the published announcement date — in one case three days early, in another about 90 minutes after an announcement time that had already passed with nothing. One arrived 56 minutes after I had checked and concluded nothing was there.

What that means for how often you check

So “the announcement is tomorrow, there is no point looking” is wrong, and it is expensive when the result is a win with a short payment window. If you are running more than a handful of entries, check on a schedule rather than on the dates the retailers published.

6. Winning starts a clock, and the clock is short

Two dated examples from the same retailer, both wins. ※ Times are as stated in the notification emails, Japan Standard Time:

21 Aug 2026, 13:00 JSTMEGA 30th Celebration box25 Aug, 16:59 JSTabout 4 days
4 Sep 2026, 15:00 JST30th Celebration nine-card set8 Sep, 16:59 JSTabout 4 days

Why four days is tighter than it sounds

Four days sounds generous until you notice that it includes a weekend, that the announcement email is in Japanese, and that store-pickup wins are worse: those give you a collection window at a named branch, typically two to seven days, with no ability to hold the item. A missed deadline is not a refund, it is a forfeited win, and the product goes back to the pool.

In my log every payment deadline on a win was met, in one case on the first day. That is the only part of this whole process that is fully under your control, so it is the part worth over-engineering.

7. What I would actually do differently

Four changes, in order of how much they saved

  • Weight entries toward channels that ship. All five of my wins were shippable first-party stores; the bulk of the losses were pickup-only chain lotteries. If you are overseas, the pickup ones are not merely long odds, they are unusable even when you win (why, in detail).
  • Do not enter the same product twice at the same first-party store. After winning one 30th Celebration item at Pokémon Center, the same account lost the identical product in the next round. This is my inference from my own log, not a published rule — but a store that wants to spread stock has an obvious reason to behave that way, and entries are a finite amount of your attention.
  • Resolve every entry within a day of its window closing. Not because losing matters, but because an open entry hides a deadline.
  • Keep a ledger with the application number. One entry in my log was recorded as “never submitted”, corrected the next day to “submitted”, and finally confirmed by the losing email quoting the application number. Without the number, that would have stayed wrong.

8. What this log does not prove

  • It is not a published win rate. No Japanese retailer discloses entry counts or allocation, so 6.1% is what one entry mix returned, not the odds of any particular lottery.
  • The mix is skewed. Popular reprints and new-set boxes are heavily over-represented, because those are the ones that go to lottery at all.
  • Wins and losses are counted from different sections of the same ledger: losses from a notification log, wins from a purchase ledger. I have stated both totals and the excluded batch above so the arithmetic can be checked rather than trusted.
  • Three months is short. I will update this page as the log grows rather than restating the same numbers.
